## Scalpellino v0.12.4b — Render, step 5b: bump maps, Phong and rim light

The second half of the materials step. Surfaces can have **bump maps**, lit the way Source
lights them, and props get **Phong** shine and **rim light**. Bakes are also much faster,
with the same light.

**Bump maps** (renders and the Camera Preview)
- A material with a `$bumpmap` (or a picture named `<texture>_normal`, `_normalmap` or
  `_bump` beside its texture) gets bumpy light: the bake fills **3 more lightmaps** for the
  face, the light along Source's 3 bump directions (direct light, sky and bounces), and the
  shader mixes them per pixel as Source SDK 2013 does (radiosity normal mapping).
- **SSBump** (`<texture>_ssbump`, or `$ssbump 1`): Source's self-shadowed bump maps, read as
  the 3 weights they hold.
- Bump maps follow **Source's convention** (green pointing down the picture). For maps made
  the other way (most tools, OpenGL), tick **%flipgreen** in the Material panel.
- `$normalmapalphaenvmapmask`: the bump map's alpha masks the cheap chrome. The reflection
  follows the bumps.
- **Terrain:** each painted layer uses its own material's bump map over the terrain's
  lightmaps.

**Props**
- A glTF model's **own normal map** is used by itself: props baked per vertex get 4 light
  values a vertex (flat and the 3 directions); props lit like a Source model light the
  bumps with their ambient cube and 2 lights.
- **Settings for a prop's material:** a `.vmt` beside the material's texture file (same
  name), or one named like the glTF material in the model's folder (`models/orb/orb.vmt`).
  It can set any VertexLitGeneric setting, another bump map included.
- **Phong** (`$phong 1`, `$phongexponent`, `$phongboost`, `$phongtint`,
  `$phongfresnelranges`, `$phongexponenttexture`, `$basemapalphaphongmask`): shine from the
  prop's 2 strongest lights, not shadowed, as Source.
- **Rim light** (`$rimlight 1`, `$rimlightexponent`, `$rimlightboost`, `$rimmask`): the
  lights and the ambient cube behind the prop, at grazing angles, as Source.

**Also**
- The **sky behind the scene** now takes the camera's exposure, like everything else in the
  render (cheap chrome no longer reflects a brighter or darker sky than the one you see).
- The Material panel's setting names no longer run into their fields (long names are cut;
  the tooltip has the whole name).
- The render log says how many faces and props got bump maps.

**Faster bakes, the same light**
- Rays are traced through a better tree (sorted by surface area, nearer side first), prop
  models are stored once and placed per prop, and a face's patches are found through a grid
  instead of one by one. The busy test maps (Normal, 2 threads): 1000 boxes 21.6 → 9.0 s,
  4000 boxes 133.9 → 56.7 s; a scene with 100 heavy props 122 → 15.6 s. Renders match the
  old ones within 2 / 255.

**Known**
- With Source SDK 2013's blend, a bump-mapped face whose bumps are flat comes out darker
  than the same face without a bump map (about 0.58 times under direct light, less in
  shade), so a seam can show where they meet. SSBumps don't do this. Whether VRAD scales the
  bump lightmaps to make up for it is to be checked in its code (with the texlight scale).
- The app's version number stays 0.12.4 (version numbers on the Mac are numbers only).
