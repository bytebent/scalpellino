## Scalpellino v0.7.0 — terrain

**Make terrain**
- Select brushes and choose **Terrain → Make terrain**. Each brush's top face becomes a
  grid of 2 to 32 cells per side. With the Texture tool you can pick any 4-cornered
  face instead, e.g. a wall.
- Run it again to change the grid size. The shape and paint are kept.

**Sculpt and paint** with the **Terrain tool (Shift+G)**
- Modes: **raise, lower, smooth, flatten** (to the height where you start), **noise**,
  **paint** (blends in the current texture) and **erase paint**.
- Radius, amount or strength, and falloff (soft, linear, hard) are typed in the
  Properties panel, with quick radius buttons. No mouse wheel.
- Drag in the 3D view, or in a 2D view looking at the terrain (top view for ground). A
  circle lying on the surface shows where you'll work.
- One drag is one undo step. Esc during a drag cancels it.
- Neighbouring patches stay joined: edges never crack, whatever you do across them.

**More**
- **Import heightmap:** a grey image, where black and white are heights you type.
- **Stitch neighbouring edges** and **Remove terrain** are in the Terrain menu.
- Moving, rotating, mirroring and scaling keep the terrain.
- Terrain is saved inside your `.map`. Other tools see the plain brush.

## Download

- **Intel Mac:** `Scalpellino-intel.zip`
- **Apple Silicon Mac (M1/M2/...):** not built automatically; run the `apple-silicon`
  workflow in the Actions tab to get one.

## First launch

Unzip to get `Scalpellino.app`. It isn't signed with an Apple developer certificate, so
macOS blocks it the first time:

1. Double-click it once (macOS refuses to open it).
2. Open **System Settings → Privacy & Security**, scroll down, click **Open Anyway**.

Or in Terminal: `xattr -dr com.apple.quarantine /path/to/Scalpellino.app`

Controls are listed in the README.
