## Scalpellino v0.7.1 — terrain fixes

**Make terrain on any face**
- **Terrain → Make terrain** now lets you pick the faces yourself: click any face in the
  3D view (Shift+click adds or removes), or click a brush in a 2D view to take its face
  toward you (top view = top face, front view = the face toward +X, and so on). Walls,
  undersides, slopes all work.
- Triangle faces can be terrain too (e.g. a wedge's slope or a box cut along its
  diagonal). Two triangles sharing their long edge join up without cracks.
- Pick the grid size in the Properties panel, press **Return** (or the button) to make
  them, **Esc** to stop picking.
- **Terrain → Terrain grid size…** changes the grid of the selected terrain.

**Vertex tool on terrain**
- With terrain selected, the vertex tool shows its points as small blue handles. Click
  one (Cmd+click adds more). In a 2D view all points stacked under the cursor come
  together, like corners.
- Drag them up or down in the 3D view or in a side view; heights snap to the grid.
  **Cmd+Shift+T** types a height (raise by, or set to).
- Neighbouring patches follow, so seams never open.
- Moving a terrain brush's corners keeps the terrain as long as its face stays flat
  (before, any vertex edit removed it). Snap to grid keeps it too.

**More textures**
- Paint up to **8 textures** on each patch (the face's own plus 7 painted). Each one
  you paint takes over its share of the spot, whatever order you painted them in.
- Painting the face's own texture uncovers it; textures with no paint left are dropped.
- The Properties panel lists the textures on the selected patch.
- Maps saved with v0.7.0 open fine.

**Fixed**
- A sculpt or paint stroke near a wall also changed the wall's terrain (it only looked
  at distances within each face). Now a stroke only reaches surfaces within its radius.

## Download

- **Intel Mac:** `Scalpellino-intel.zip`
- **Apple Silicon Mac (M1/M2/...):** not built automatically; run the `apple-silicon`
  workflow in the Actions tab to get one.

## First launch

Unzip to get `Scalpellino.app`. It isn't signed with an Apple developer certificate, so
macOS blocks it the first time:

1. Double-click it once (macOS refuses to open it).
2. Open **System Settings → Privacy & Security**, scroll down, click **Open Anyway**.

Or in Terminal: `xattr -dr com.apple.quarantine /path/to/Scalpellino.app`

Controls are listed in the README.
